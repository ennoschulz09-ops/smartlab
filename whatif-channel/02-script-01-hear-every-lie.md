# Skript 01: What If You Could Hear Every Lie Told Near You?

Länge: ca. 1.600 gesprochene Wörter (ca. 9:30 bis 10:00 Min. mit Pausen und Effekten). Du-Form, Gegenwart.
Hinweis: Das erste Skript ist ein Entwurf. Beim Einsprechen Pausen (Glocke!) einplanen, das streckt die Laufzeit auf ca. 10:15.
Figuren: DU, SAM (bester Freund), NORA (kleine Schwester, 9), MR. VANCE (Gegenspieler, Bauunternehmer).
Fähigkeit: Du hörst ein tiefes Glockenläuten, wenn jemand in 10 Metern Entfernung bewusst lügt.

---

## [COLD OPEN 0:00]

*Bild: Autohaus, Mittag, Glocke als Soundeffekt.*

A bell rings.

Low. Deep. Like a church bell underwater.

"One previous owner," says the salesman, patting the hood.

*Bong.*

You smile. "Funny. The report says four."

His face falls. The price falls with it. Two thousand dollars, gone in ten seconds.

Sam stares at you from the passenger seat. "Okay. How did you do that?"

You tell him the truth. Since this morning, you can hear one thing. Every time someone near you tells a lie on purpose, a bell rings. Only you can hear it.

## [REGELN 0:40]

Three rules.

One. The bell rings for lies, not for mistakes. If someone is wrong but honest, it stays silent.

Two. It only works within ten meters.

Three. It tells you that someone is lying. It never tells you the truth.

And one day, you'll need that last rule more than any other.

But right now, you're in a parking lot, holding a car that costs two thousand dollars less than it should, and the only thing you want is to see how far this goes.

## [MINI-ERFOLGE 1:25]

*Bild: Szenenwechsel alle 15 bis 20 Sek.*

Poker night. Five friends, a pile of chips, a pot that keeps growing.

Dan pushes everything in. "I've got a monster."

*Bong.*

You call. He shows a pair of twos. You win with a pair of fours.

Fourteen dollars. Worth it.

Here's the thing. Most people think they're good at spotting lies. They aren't. In studies, people catch a lie only about fifty-four percent of the time. That's barely better than flipping a coin.

You don't flip coins anymore.

Job interview next. The manager smiles. "We'll definitely call you back."

*Bong.*

You thank her anyway. You walk out calm. No waiting by the phone, no refreshing your email. You already know.

Then the gym. A trainer says, "Anyone can do this program, no experience needed."

*Bong.*

You cancel the membership and save four hundred dollars a year.

It feels like cheating. In a way, it is. Every conversation is a game, and you can see the cards.

Saturday, the flea market. A man sells a watch. "Genuine. Belonged to a pilot."

*Bong.*

"Twenty dollars," you say.

"Thirty!"

"Twenty. And I'll tell you where you actually found it."

He stares. You don't know where he found it. You only know the pilot story is fake. But his face tells you the rest, and he hands over the watch for twenty.

Then, on the walk home, you spot a wallet on the sidewalk. Cash inside, a driver's license, a photo of a dog. You knock on the nearest door. A woman opens.

"Oh, that's mine," she says.

*Bong.*

It isn't. You walk to the right house, three doors down, and a very confused old man hugs a wallet like it's a lost child.

That's the first time the bell does something good.

You start to think maybe this is a gift after all. Maybe you could fix everything. Every scam, every fake smile, every fake promise. You could be the one honest ear in a world of liars.

Sam takes it further. "Dude. Ask the bank. Ask the mayor. Ask your teacher what she really thinks of your essay."

You laugh. It's a good laugh. It's the last easy one for a while.

## [KOSTEN 3:10]

But the bell doesn't only ring for the bad people.

Mom, at breakfast, on a Monday. "I'm fine, honey."

*Bong.*

Your cousin, at a family dinner. "I love the sweater."

*Bong.*

Your neighbor. "Great to see you!"

*Bong.*

It turns out almost everyone lies every day. In one famous study, people reported about one or two lies per day on average. Most of them are tiny. "I'm on my way." "No worries." "I'm fine."

And here's the strange part. Researchers found that most of those lies come from a small group of people who lie a lot. Most of us lie much less than you'd think.

That's the real world.

Your world has a bell.

The mall becomes a nightmare. Hundreds of small bells, layered like rain. At night, you wear headphones just to sleep.

## [DAS GESETZTE DETAIL 3:55]

There's one person who never rings.

Nora. Your little sister. Nine years old. She tells you about her day every night, and the bell never makes a sound. Not once.

She says, "You're my favorite person."

Silence.

You don't think about it. Not yet. But somewhere in the back of your head, you file it away.

## [VERTRAUEN BRICHT 4:30]

A week later, you can't stop listening.

Sam hands you a birthday present. A scarf. Ugly. Green, with orange stripes.

"I picked it just for you. I hope you love it."

*Bong.*

Not the first part. The second. He doesn't hope you love it. He knows you won't.

And somehow, that hurts more than a thousand big lies.

You say thank you. "I love it," you add.

No bell. Your own lies never ring. That's the cruel part. You can hear everyone else's, and your own simply feel normal.

Then your mom. A Tuesday phone call. "No, of course I'm not worried about you."

*Bong.*

She is worried. Of course she is. She's your mom. But the lie wasn't meant to trick you. It was meant to protect you. And now you know it, and you can't un-know it.

That's the thing nobody tells you about the truth. Sometimes it doesn't set you free. Sometimes it just sits in the room with you.

Psychologists call these "prosocial lies", the small ones we tell to be kind. Most people tell them without a second thought, and most of the time, they hold relationships together like glue.

You've pulled out the glue.

Your best friend's band plays a show. You stand in the back, and when he finishes he asks, "Was it good?"

"It was great," you say. And you almost believe yourself. Almost. Because the guitarist next to him whispers to the drummer, "That was a disaster," and the bell rings so loudly you flinch.

By the end of the month, you stop asking people how they are. You already know the answer. You stop going to dinners. You stop calling. You eat lunch in your car with the radio on, because radio hosts are far away, and far away is quiet.

Sam notices first. "You've been weird lately."

"I'm fine."

You are not fine.

## [WENDEPUNKT 6:00]

Then the town council meeting.

*Bild: Saal, 200 Leute, Projektor.*

The old dam above your neighborhood needs repairs. A company wants to postpone them for a year to save money. Mr. Vance, the contractor, stands at the microphone in a perfect suit.

"The structure is completely safe."

*Bong.*

The room applauds. The council votes in forty minutes.

You turn to Sam. "He's lying. The dam isn't safe."

"How do you know?"

"I just know."

He looks at you. Really looks. Then he says what nobody has said to you in a month.

"Okay. Then what do we do?"

## [KAMPF GEGEN DIE UHR 7:20]

Here's the problem. A bell is not proof. If you stand up and say, "I heard a bell," you'll be a story at dinner, nothing more.

So you think about rule three.

The bell only tells you that someone is lying. It never tells you the truth. But it can tell you something else: which questions he's scared of.

You raise your hand.

"Mr. Vance. When was the last independent inspection?"

"Last spring."

*Bong.*

"And what did it find?"

"Nothing of concern."

*Bong.*

"Then you won't mind if the council requests the report today."

Silence. His smile freezes. Then, for the first time in the whole meeting, he doesn't say a word.

The room notices. You see it. It's like a crack running across glass.

Sam leans in. "Keep going. Ask him something he can't dodge."

You think. Mr. Vance has a lawyer's face and a salesman's voice. He doesn't lie badly. He lies fast. The trick is to slow him down.

"Mr. Vance, do you live in this neighborhood?"

"I do."

Silence. No bell. It's true. He lives right below the dam.

"Then you'd sleep fine tonight, knowing your own family lives under it?"

The room turns. He looks at the council. He looks at you. He looks, for a second, at his own hands.

"Of course," he says.

*Bong.*

It's the quietest bell yet. But it's the one that matters.

You've learned something. A liar can fake a fact, but it's much harder to fake a feeling, especially when you ask about the people he loves.

A council member leans forward. "Mr. Vance? The report?"

He opens his mouth.

Three seconds.

*Bong.*

"It's... being finalized."

*Bong.*

"Almost ready."

And the bell keeps ringing. Seven times. Eight. The sound is so loud now that you have to hold your head.

But the room can't hear it. They only see a man who can't answer a simple question.

The vote is cancelled.

## [PAY-OFF 9:10]

An inspector arrives three days later. He finds cracks along the base of the dam, wide enough to slide a hand into.

The repairs begin the next week.

Sam never asks how you knew. He just sits next to you on the bus again, and for the first time in a month you don't hear a single bell.

That night, a storm rolls in. Thunder shakes the house. Nora appears in your doorway, hugging a stuffed rabbit.

"I'm not scared," she says.

*Bong.*

The faintest bell you've ever heard. Soft. Almost kind.

She was never silent because she never lied. She was silent because she never had a reason to.

Until tonight.

You lift the blanket. She climbs in.

"Don't worry," you whisper. "Everything's going to be fine."

You don't hear a bell. Your own lies never ring.

But you know.

And you hold her until she falls asleep.

## [OUTRO 10:00]

So, would you want to hear every lie? Even the small ones, like "I love the sweater"?

Tell us in the comments. And if you'd have asked that question at the council meeting, hit like and subscribe for the next what-if.
